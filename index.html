Commodity price forecaster

Weekly forecasts for nine energy, metal, and grain futures. The question I scored: at one week ahead, do ARIMA or Prophet beat a naïve forecast?

01

Data

Yahoo Finance daily closes for WTI, Brent, natural gas, gold, silver, copper, corn, wheat, and soybeans, taken 30 Sep 2026 and resampled to weeks. Weather from Open-Meteo is context only. It is not in the score.

02

Test

52 one-step weeks, 5 Oct 2025 to 27 Sep 2026. At each week the model is refit on earlier data only. I score last-value, drift, a 52-week seasonal naïve, ARIMA(1,1,1), and Prophet with MAE, sMAPE, and MASE.

03

What happened

Drift won, average MASE 2.204, tied with last-value at 2.207. ARIMA was close at 2.255. Prophet was 8.037. Seasonal naïve was last at 16.414. A simple baseline won.

Drift 2.204 · Naïve 2.207 · ARIMA 2.255
Prophet 8.037 · Seasonal naïve 16.414
04

Limits

MASE above 1 here means the test year moved more than the history from 2016, especially in gold and silver. It does not mean the code broke. ARIMA’s nominal 95% intervals covered the next week 79.5% of the time overall, and 44.2% of the time for gold. They were too narrow. This is a study, not trading advice.

DCF Valuation Model

CodeLive demo

Question

How far are AAPL, MSFT, KO, JNJ, CAT, and XOM from a plain DCF, and what growth does the price imply?

Method

Free cash flow to the firm from SEC XBRL 10-Ks. Revenue growth fades over 10 years. WACC uses a Blume-adjusted 5-year beta, a 5.13% Treasury yield (FRED DGS10), and a 4.23% Damodaran equity risk premium. I also run a sensitivity grid, a 10,000-draw Monte Carlo, and a reverse DCF. The TypeScript port matches Python within 0.01%.

Findings

The model is below the market for most names. AAPL is $122.18 against $348.22, and CAT is $279.82 against $704.05. XOM is the only one above its price ($216.26 vs $178.23), and 71.9% of the Monte Carlo draws put value above the price. The reverse DCF says the prices imply about 20.1% 10-year revenue growth for AAPL and 20.3% for CAT. I left that gap as it is.

UNH Equity Pitch

CodeLive demo

Question

Is there a long case for UnitedHealth at $367.08 on 30 Sep 2026? The name came off a scored screen.

Method

A two-stage FCF DCF. Bear, base, and bull growth come from the stock's own rolling FCF history. The discount rate is a CAPM cost of equity. I also fit Fama-French five factors plus momentum, look at peer comps, and test a walk-forward trend signal after 10 bp of costs. The files are frozen and hashed.

Findings

The base case is $526.64, about 43.5% above the price, in a range of $354.47 to $764.68. The price implies 6.0% stage-1 FCF growth, against 13.7% in the base case. With the full-sample beta of 0.78, the value falls to $321.29, 12.5% below the price. The trend signal (Sharpe 0.29) and buy-and-hold (0.49) both trail SPY (0.87).

Energy Sector Deep Dive

CodeLive demo

Question

How do five US oil and gas segments trade against oil, and does anything else help once you know that?

Method

Eighteen companies from 2015 on. Oil betas use HAC standard errors. Fundamentals are dated to the SEC filing, not the period end. I cluster on those fundamentals and run a weekly factor model walk-forward against an expanding mean. Companies that did not survive are listed in the config.

Findings

Producers are the most oil-sensitive segment (beta 0.55 per 1% WTI move, HAC t = 9.1) and refiners the least (0.27). Producers fell 78.7% in the 2020 crash. Clustering mostly recovers the segments (adjusted Rand index 0.61). Same-week factors explain 53.3% to 67.6% of segment returns out of sample. Last week's factors do not: out-of-sample R² is −3.7% to −7.7%.

Portfolio Risk Lab

CodeLive demo

Question

Do mean-variance, risk parity, or Black-Litterman beat a simple benchmark on 21 ETFs, once the test is walk-forward and costs are paid?

Method

Each month's weights use only the prior 60 months. Covariance is shrunk halfway to its diagonal. Long-only, 30% cap, 10 bp per unit of turnover. I report historical 95% VaR and CVaR, drawdowns, and a Fama-French five-factor plus momentum regression. The in-browser allocator is tested against the Python numbers.

Findings

From May 2012 to Sep 2026, no optimizer beat SPY (Sharpe 0.92) or 60/40 (0.84). Mean-variance reached 0.76. Risk parity cut max drawdown to −12.72% from −23.93% for SPY, with volatility of 5.45% versus 14.03%. No strategy has a significant alpha. Mean-variance is −2.20% a year, t = −1.63.

SPY Volatility Surface

CodeLive demo

Question

What does one real SPY option chain imply for volatility, and can an SVI curve fit it without arbitrage?

Method

Black-Scholes-Merton, with Brent root-finding for implied vol. Quotes are out-of-the-money prints from the 22 Oct 2026 close. The rate is 4.02% and the dividend yield is 1.22%. Each expiry gets its own raw SVI fit, then a butterfly check and a calendar check.

Findings

The snapshot has 1,077 quotes across 11 expiries, from 7 to 603 days. ATM implied vol goes from 15.75% at 7 days to 18.39% at 603 days, and is lowest at 14.52% (39 days). Put skew falls from 26.12 to 3.24 vol points. SVI error is 0.22 to 0.67 vol points, and all 11 slices pass the butterfly check. 49 of 1,210 calendar points fail, all outside the quoted strikes.

Volatility Forecasting (HAR vs ML)

CodeLive demo

Question

Can gradient boosting or a small LSTM forecast next-day realized volatility for SPY and nine sector ETFs better than HAR-RV and GARCH(1,1)?

Method

Walk-forward, 3,473 out-of-sample days per ETF, 2 Jan 2013 to 22 Oct 2026. I score QLIKE and MSE, and run Diebold-Mariano tests against HAR. The README figures are written from results/summary.json.

Findings

On QLIKE the LSTM is best on all 10 ETFs (mean 0.2968 vs 0.3262 for HAR, −9.0%) and significantly better on 8 of 10. Gradient boosting is level with HAR (−0.3%). GARCH is worse, significantly so on 7. On MSE, nothing is significantly different from HAR. The worst five days are 40% to 94% of HAR's squared error, so the LSTM edge is not a better call on the spikes.

Portfolio Risk & Performance Analyzer

CodeLive demo

Question

How does a six-ETF portfolio (SPY, EFA, AGG, TLT, GLD, VNQ) compare with holding SPY, from 2006 to 2026?

Method

Total returns from a hashed Yahoo snapshot, loaded into SQLite. Eleven SQL queries are checked against pandas. Equal weight is rebalanced monthly. Minimum variance is re-estimated each month from the trailing 252 days only. Both pay 10 bp one-way costs.

Findings

From 4 Jan 2006 to 30 Sep 2026, equal weight cut volatility from 19.20% to 11.11% and max drawdown from −55.2% to −32.2%, and gave up about four points of return a year (7.08% vs 11.07%). Sharpe was 0.52 vs 0.55. Minimum variance, from Feb 2007, averaged 88% in AGG, with the lowest volatility (5.38%) and the lowest Sharpe (0.35). Lower risk, not a better Sharpe.
